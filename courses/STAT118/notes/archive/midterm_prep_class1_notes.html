Dataset Options

For this assignment you will choose one of two datasets.

Environmental Justice Index (EJI)

The Environmental Justice Index combines environmental, demographic, and health data to measure environmental burdens experienced by communities across the United States. The data are organized at the level of census tracts, which are small geographic areas used by the U.S. Census Bureau.

The dataset includes information about environmental exposures, social vulnerability, and health outcomes.

Some questions someone might explore with this dataset include:

  • Which regions experience the highest environmental burden?
  • Do some environmental indicators tend to occur together?
  • Are there geographic patterns in environmental justice measures?

Youth Risk Behavior Survey (YRBS)

The Youth Risk Behavior Survey collects information from high school students about behaviors related to health and well-being. The survey includes questions about things like physical activity, mental health, substance use, and other behaviors.

The dataset also includes demographic information such as grade level and sex.

Some questions someone might explore with this dataset include:

  • Do certain behaviors differ across grade levels?
  • Are some behaviors related to others?
  • Are there differences between demographic groups?

Exploratory Data Analysis

The process we will practice today is called exploratory data analysis (EDA).

EDA is the process of using summaries and visualizations to discover patterns in data and develop questions about those patterns.

Useful quote from a random person on Reddit (onearmedecon)

The primary goal of EDA is to explore the underlying structure, patterns, relationships, and anomalies within the data, allowing the analyst to make informed decisions and develop appropriate models or strategies for further analysis.

This usually involves some combination of the following:

  1. Cleaning (e.g., removing duplicates)
  2. Transformations (e.g., aggregation)
  3. Descriptive/univariate analysis (e.g., summary statistics)
  4. Correlational/bivariate analysis (e.g., correlation matrices, simple hypothesis testing)
  5. Visualizations (e.g., histograms, scatter plots)

The workflow usually looks something like this:

→ Understand the dataset
→ Ask questions
→ Create plots
→ Identify patterns
→ Interpret what those patterns might mean

This process is iterative.

Today we will walk through the first part of this process.

Step 1: Understanding the Dataset

Before we make any plots, we need to understand what the dataset contains.

Some useful questions to ask when you first encounter a dataset include:

  • What does each row represent?
  • What variables are included in the dataset?
  • What do those variables measure?
  • What if any data cleaning is needed?
  • Which variables seem interesting to explore?

Step 2: Brainstorming Questions

Now that we have looked at the structure of the dataset, the next step is to think about questions we might ask.

Some common types of exploratory questions include:

  • Do groups differ from one another?
  • Are two variables related?
  • What are the highest or lowest values?
  • How does something change across categories?

Step 5: Identify and describe patterns

When we look at a plot, the first step is to describe what we see.

Examples of descriptive observations include:

  • One group has higher values than another group
  • The values increase across categories
  • There appears to be a relationship between two variables
  • Try to focus on what the data show, rather than explaining why.

As a group, discuss the patterns in the plots we just made:

  • What does the plot show?
  • What doesn’t it show?
  • What questions does it answer?
  • What questions does it raise?

Step 6: Interpreting meaning

After identifying a pattern, we can begin to think about what it might mean.

Possible questions to consider:

  • What might explain the pattern we see?
  • Are there limitations in the data that affect our interpretation?
  • What additional questions does this pattern raise?

These kinds of ideas are what you will discuss in the Discussion section of your report.

Discuss answers to these questions with your group.
